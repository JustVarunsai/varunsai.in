THE NOTEBOOK / THREE SMALL PRINCIPLES

Thoughts in
the margins.

A few principles behind the way I want to build. Short enough to keep in mind when the work gets complicated.

IN THIS NOTEBOOK01 · The next action02 · A way out03 · The waiting
01 / PRODUCT THINKING

Start with the next action.

Before choosing the model, the framework, or the screen, I want to know what someone is trying to do next.

“Answer questions about our documents” is a capability. “Help a new teammate find the right onboarding step” is a direction. That difference changes what needs to be retrieved, what an answer should look like, and whether AI is useful at all.

The first version can be small: one person, one recurring question, one answer they can check. Watch what happens after the answer. That is where the product starts to become clear.

A question I keep nearby: what will someone be able to do after this that they couldn’t do before?

02 / HUMAN × AI

Give automation an exit.

An automation should have a good answer to “wait, that’s wrong.”

That might mean an original document beside an extracted field, a review queue for uncertain messages, or a way to undo a changed label. The useful part is giving the person enough context and control to recover.

I like separating a suggestion from an action. Suggesting a reply is different from sending it. Showing a possible answer is different from updating a record. Each boundary deserves a deliberate decision.

Make the correction path as easy to find as the happy path.
03 / THE SMALL DETAILS

Make the waiting useful.

A system can be doing the right thing and still leave someone wondering whether anything is happening.

A useful waiting state explains what is underway, preserves what the person entered, and gives them a way to stop. If there is a delay, the interface should help them decide what to do with it.

Small touches can bring some warmth, too. The little guide on this site follows a scroll and then settles down. It is there when you want a detour, with a pause button when you don’t.

Personality is welcome. The person using the product still gets the last word.
